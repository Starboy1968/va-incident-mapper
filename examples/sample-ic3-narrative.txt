KEY - PLAIN LANGUAGE (FOR NON-LAWYERS)
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
This section explains, in plain English, what the flags and statute
citations in this report mean. Not legal advice.

WHAT THE MESSAGE FLAGS MEAN
  CONTACT AFTER BLOCK OR NEW ACCOUNTS
    References being blocked, or contacts the recipient from new accounts.

  PERMANENCE DECLARATION
    Says the contact will never end or cannot be escaped.

  SURVEILLANCE OR TRACKING CLAIM
    Says the sender has been watching, tracking or knows where the recipient is.

  COERCIVE CONTACT DEMAND
    Insists the recipient respond, unblock or comply.

WHY FEELINGS ARE INCLUDED
  Stalking and harassment laws often ask whether the conduct caused a
  reasonable person real fear or distress. The reporting party's own account
  of how they felt at the time is a first-hand record of that. It is not a
  diagnosis.

STATUTES REFERENCED
  Va. Code § 18.2-60
    Knowingly communicating, in writing (including email, text or social media
    post), a threat to kill or do bodily injury to a person, placing that person
    in reasonable apprehension of death or bodily harm.

  18 U.S.C. § 875(c) and (d) *
    Transmitting in interstate commerce a threat to injure a person (subsection
    c), or a threat to injure property, reputation or accuse of a crime with
    intent to extort (subsection d).

  Va. Code § 18.2-152.7:1
    Using a computer or network to communicate obscene, vulgar, profane, lewd or
    indecent language, obscene suggestions, or threats of illegal or immoral
    acts, with intent to coerce, intimidate or harass.

  Va. Code § 18.2-60.3
    Conduct directed at another person on more than one occasion, by any means
    including electronic communication, with intent to place the person in
    reasonable fear of death, criminal sexual assault or bodily injury to
    themselves or family.

  18 U.S.C. § 2261A(2) *
    Using electronic communication or any interactive computer service in a
    course of conduct, with intent to harass or intimidate, that places a person
    in reasonable fear of death or serious injury or causes substantial
    emotional distress.

  18 U.S.C. § 1030 *
    Accessing a protected computer without authorization or exceeding authorized
    access, damaging computers, trafficking in passwords, and extortion
    involving computers. After Van Buren v. United States (2021), 'exceeds
    authorized access' is read narrowly: it covers accessing areas of a system
    that are off-limits to you, not merely misusing access you were given or
    breaking a terms-of-service rule.

  18 U.S.C. § 2703(f) *
    Asks a platform to preserve records (IP addresses, account data, messages)
    once law enforcement requests it, so they are not deleted.

* Federal citation not independently re-checked. Virginia citations checked 2026-10-05.
